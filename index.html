Section 02

Things that probably slow teams like this down

Not dramatic failures. Just quiet drags on speed.

01

Good conversations getting buried

High-intent DMs disappear under low-signal ones. No triage system means no prioritisation. The best leads wait the longest.

02

Founders answering the same questions manually

Pricing, onboarding, "how does this work" — these have answers. Writing them fresh every time is a tax on the person who should be building.

03

Context switching between channels

LinkedIn, email, Slack, support — each one is its own mental mode. Without a unified view, things fall through and momentum breaks.

04

Customer ops becoming reactive

When support is the only process, you're always catching up. A proactive ops layer surfaces issues before they become customer complaints.

05

Support replies losing personality at scale

Templates help speed but kill tone. The fix isn't fewer templates — it's building ones that actually sound human. That's a systems problem.

Section 05

Things I enjoy fixing

Not problems I tolerate. Things I actively look for.

Slow workflows

If something takes longer than it should, there's usually a reason. Finding that reason is the fun part.

Repetitive tasks

Anything done more than twice deserves a second look. Either automate it or make it faster to do manually.

Confusing systems

Good systems don't need a manual. If the manual is long, the system needs work.

Poor customer experiences

Usually a symptom of internal ops problems. Fix the back-end, the front-end improves automatically.

Operations bottlenecks

The places where work piles up. Founder time, team bandwidth, unclear ownership. These are fixable.
